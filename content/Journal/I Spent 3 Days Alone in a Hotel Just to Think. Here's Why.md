Not a vacation. Not a retreat. Just time to think.

After leaving my Associate Director role, I decided to take some personal time over the next few months. Moving into AI has been my long-term goal, and I wanted to use this time well. So the first thing I did wasn't coding or job hunting. I booked a hotel room for three days, alone. My goal was simple: plan the next three months, both my AI learning and my personal life, and how the two would fit together. (Big thanks to my wife for approving this ‘business trip.’ 😄)

**Where the idea came from.** I'd read about Bill Gates' "Think Week," time spent alone to step back and think about what matters most. Mine was smaller and simpler. No cabin, no stack of research papers, just one book, _AI Engineering_ by Chip Huyen, and quiet time to think about my own plan.

**Why a hotel, and not home.** I could have done this at home, but I know myself. Small things like laundry and chores always pull my attention. A hotel room removed all of that. No routines, no distractions, just me, a reference book on the subject, a notebook, and the plan.

Being based in Sri Lanka, I'm lucky to have some beautiful beach hotels nearby. Here's a short clip of the view from my room. Not a bad place to sit and think for three days.

![[thinkweek.mp4]]

**Thinking beyond learning.** Learning plans are easy to make on paper. Real life doesn't pause for them. So I also spent time thinking about my personal life and the things that needed my attention over these months, and how my learning could fit around them instead of competing with them. I wanted a plan I could actually keep, not one that only worked if everything else stood still.

**What came out of it.** By the end of the three days, I had a clear three/four-month plan: a step-by-step path for learning AI properly, with real systems and real practice, and a realistic way to balance it with my personal commitments. Nothing fancy, just something simple enough to actually follow, starting Monday.

If you're at a turning point and keep making decisions in bits and pieces around a busy schedule, I'd really recommend taking even one full day away, somewhere with zero distractions, before deciding what's next.
