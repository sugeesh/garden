title: Why I Left My Associate Director Role and Changed Direction to AI
modified: 2026-07-19
---

I recently left my full-time Associate Director role and I'm taking some personal time over the next few months. Moving into AI has been my long-term goal for a while, so I'm using this time to focus fully on learning and building in AI. Here's the honest reasoning behind it.

**Why now.** AI is moving fast. I'm an experienced backend engineer, but I've never properly studied how LLMs work. To really learn it, I need deep focus and time, not a few spare hours after work.

**Why I couldn't do both.** I tried to learn AI while working full-time. It didn't work well. I wasn't giving my best to either one, and I didn't want my job to suffer. Having this break gives me the time to focus on AI properly, which is an added bonus.

**What I'm seeing in the industry.** A lot of people are adding AI to their products without really understanding how it works. They copy and paste, connect an API, and call it done. I'm not saying this to sound superior; I've been tempted to do the same. But I realized it doesn't give me the enjoyment I've always found in coding and system design. I want to actually understand how these systems work, even if it takes longer.

**Why this matters to me.** These days, anyone can build something with AI. That's not hard anymore. What I want is different: to be an engineer who understands AI systems, not just someone who ships fast without knowing why things work or when they'll break.

**What's next.** I'm going back to basics and rebuilding my AI knowledge properly: real systems, real practice, no shortcuts. Part of this is upgrading my homelab, so I have a place to test, break, and actually learn things hands-on.
